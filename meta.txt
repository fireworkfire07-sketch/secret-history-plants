The Oldest Medicine on Earth | The Secret History of Peyote
It is small, soft, and spineless — so plain you could walk right past it. Yet this humble desert cactus is the oldest plant medicine ever chemically confirmed by science: peyote buttons found in a Texas cave were radiocarbon-dated to nearly 5,700 years old and STILL contained their active compound. This is the full story of peyote (Lophophora williamsii): the sacred cactus of the Huichol people, who still walk a 500km pilgrimage to hunt it as a sacred deer; the Native American Church that fought for centuries to defend it; and the German chemist who, in 1897, isolated its secret — mescaline.

And where ancient healers saw sacred medicine, modern science is now looking again: peyote is listed in the Mexican pharmacopoeia, long used traditionally for fevers, pain, and disease, and members of the Native American Church have reported using it to overcome alcoholism — a claim researchers are now taking seriously, alongside studies of mescaline for depression, anxiety, and addiction. The ancient healers, it seems, grasped something real.

By the end, one question is answered: why did humans hold this small cactus sacred for nearly 6,000 years — and what did they know that science is only now confirming?

History, mystery, shamanic tradition, and real, verified science — hidden in the oldest medicine on Earth.
