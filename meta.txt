The Spice Worth More Than Gold — And the 1,000-Year Lie That Hid It | The Secret History of Cinnamon
For 2,000 years, one spice was worth more than gold. Emperor Nero burned a year's supply of it at his wife's funeral. Explorers crossed oceans to find its source — and empires went to war over a single island. To protect that secret, traders invented a lie so wild it lasted a thousand years: that cinnamon was guarded by giant birds nesting on unclimbable cliffs. This is the full story of the peeled bark that shaped history — from Egyptian tombs to the funeral fires of Rome, from Herodotus's cinnamon birds to the colonial wars for Sri Lanka.

And hidden inside that same bark, modern science found cinnamaldehyde — with clinical trials showing cinnamon can lower blood sugar — plus a hidden danger the ancients never knew: the coumarin in common cassia cinnamon, which can harm the liver in high doses.

By the end, one question is answered: were the ancient healers right about cinnamon — and what did they never know?

History, mystery, and real, verified science — hidden in a spice sitting in your kitchen right now.
