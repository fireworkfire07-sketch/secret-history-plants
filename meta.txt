The Money You Could Eat | The Secret History of Chocolate
Once, there was a currency that grew on trees — worth more than gold, counted out one bean at a time, and so valuable that people counterfeited it out of clay. It was cacao: the "food of the gods," so sacred the Aztec emperor Montezuma allegedly drank 50 cups a day from golden goblets. This is the full story of the bitter seed that became money, medicine, and chocolate — from Maya creation myths and Aztec markets where a turkey cost 100 beans, to the Spanish conquistador who called it "a drink for pigs" before Europe sweetened it into a global obsession.

And hidden inside that same seed, modern science found the truth behind the reverence: theobromine, the gentle stimulant named "food of the gods," and cocoa flavanols shown in clinical trials to lower blood pressure and lift mood — proving the ancients were right all along.

By the end, one question is answered: why does chocolate feel so good — and were the Aztecs right to call it divine?

History, mystery, and real, verified science — hidden in a bar of chocolate you already love.
